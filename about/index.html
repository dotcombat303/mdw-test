About the Project

The digital economy has reconfigured relationships between technology and labour. Workers involved in the production and maintenance of the digital economy are subjected to intensifying exploitative social dynamics of caste, class and gender and experience new forms of precarity and inequality.

The challenge of the grant is to support the development of aesthetic modes that engage and respond to these conditions. The grantee projects bridge artistic research and academic inquiry towards new formations of evidence, critique and action.

Three research-based projects by Anisha Baid, Debarun Sarkar, Cheshta Arora and Gabriele de Seta, and Yatharth were selected via open call in March 2025.

The grantees participate in a curatorial mentorship programme led by Mila T Samdub. The projects will culminate in the launch of a publication in summer 2026.

The grant was preceded by an academic workshop in Bangalore in March 2025 that explored contemporary concerns in data labour from India. The Mapping Data Work grant was conceived by Jef Ausloos and Divij Joshi and is supported by the University of Amsterdam’s Institute for Information Law.
