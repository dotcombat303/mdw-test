Mapping Data Work supported research based creative media projects to map the digital economy in India from the perspectives of the labour that supports and sustains it.

The digital economy is reconfiguring relationships between technology and labour. Workers involved in the production and maintenance of the digital economy are subjected to intensifying exploitative social dynamics of caste, class and gender and experience new forms of precarity and inequality. Mapping Data Work supported the development of aesthetic modes that engage and respond to these conditions. The grantee projects bridge artistic research and academic inquiry towards new formations of evidence, critique and action.

Three projects were selected via open call in March 2025. The selected practitioners were:

  • Anisha Baid
  • Debarun Sarkar, Cheshta Arora, Gabriele de Seta and Luke Drozd
  • Yatharth

The grantees participated in a curatorial mentorship programme led by Mila T Samdub. Several guests responded to and critiqued the projects over the course of their development:

  • Srravya Chandhiramowuli
  • Aditya Singh
  • Aditi Surie
  • Vladan Joler
  • Marialaura Ghidini

The grant was preceded by an interdisciplinary academic workshop in Bengaluru in March 2025 that explored contemporary concerns in data labour from India. The workshop was organized by the University of Amsterdam’s Institute for Information Law, Tattle, and Aapti Institute.

A launch event, titled “Refracting Data Work: Between Art and Policy”, was hosted at Bengaluru’s Museum of Art and Photography on 18 March, 2025.

The Mapping Data Work project was conceived by Jef Ausloos and Divij Joshi and is supported by the University of Amsterdam’s Institute for Information Law.
